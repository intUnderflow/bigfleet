# Complexity audit — June 2026

**Question (author's):** "This is becoming an extremely complex system — is that actually justified versus the papers?"

**Inputs:** two independent audits run 2026-06: (1) a four-subsystem complexity audit (Same/gang engine, scaletest harness, engine core, validation-machinery ROI); (2) a wide system-vs-papers audit (full normative-surface extraction from both papers — 47 MUSTs, 15 MUST-NOTs, 11 delegations, 24 silences — seven per-subsystem divergence audits, and a minimal-faithful counterfactual implementation sized at ~7.1K LOC). This document reconciles them. LOC figures are non-test Go unless stated: engine ~14.9K, harness 7.6K Go + 3.4K YAML, tests 18K, generated proto 5.8K. The papers are 303 lines; there are 43 ADRs.

---

## 1. Verdict

Mostly, yes — and where it isn't, the causes are specific and fixable. Of ~44K hand-written lines, roughly 85% is either the papers' machine rendered faithfully, bought by a measured scale wall, or bought by a recorded incident with numbers attached. The ~15% (~6.5K LOC) that is unforced is not where intuition points: it is not the Same/gang engine core, which is coherence work that any strict single-domain Same semantics forces. It is superseded migration halves the harness never deleted, a suppression layer (the ADR-0042 parking machinery) built to silence demand the catalog fabricates, and dead scaffolding that survived milestone cutovers without a remnants sweep. Meanwhile the system is under-built on a handful of paper MUSTs — shard→provider fencing, the provider dial-out path, Phase 3's Idle→Speculative release half, and a headline bind-latency SLO that is silently unmeasured on every active profile. The mechanisms are justified almost without exception; the retention isn't.

### How the two audits were reconciled

The audits disagreed in four places. Resolutions, with the stronger evidence:

**(a) The ADR-0042 parking layer.** The wide audit's shard-spine review classified parking "proportionate" — it is incident-anchored (#55/#57, churn 27/sec → 9/sec → ~0), the constants are bounded and documented. The four-subsystem audit dug one level deeper and traced the *incident's demand* to a fabricated catalog entry: `gpu-training-large` demands 64–256 rack-coherent whole-GPU nodes against racks of ~52 machines — demand no physical topology can satisfy and that real systems place at zone/pod scope — and the harness had to fabricate 256-machine single-rack seed blocks to make it even representable. ADR-0042's own consequences section defers the rack-capacity question while shipping the suppression machinery. The wide audit's *harness* review independently corroborates (it flags the same catalog entry and the 256-machine blocks as artifact-driven). **Resolution: the parking layer moves from incident-earned to unforced-conditional.** It is genuinely load-bearing against today's fabricated demand (deleting it now re-opens ~9/sec churn), so its deletion is gated, not blind — see §4 action 4. Both audits agree on the gate.

**(b) The counterfactual's "three largest unforced pools" vs the subsystem verdicts.** The counterfactual nominated AvailableCapacity confidence pipeline (~400), coordinator admin/ops surface (~300), and the all-in-one subcommand (~194). The subsystem audits nominate much larger pools: harness superseded halves (~1.6K Go + 1.55K YAML), the ADR-0042 layer (~500 engine + test share), dead scaffolding (~1K). The discrepancy has two causes: the counterfactual scoped unforced to engine-only and declared the harness "wholly scale-earned" (too generous — the harness audits show ~20% of harness Go is the superseded half of already-shipped replacements), and it took incidents at face value (so parking landed in incident-earned). The counterfactual's three pools are also the *weakest* candidates on their own merits: AvailableCapacity production is paper-optional (C6) but the CRD is paper-required and the rate-limit layer has a recorded trigger (500 writes/sec into kine); the admin surface is ADR-0008-anchored to a runbook need; all-in-one is the quickstart. **Resolution: §3 uses the subsystem audits' pools; the counterfactual's ~0.9K stays counted inside engine-unforced but none of its three makes the top three.**

**(c) Inventory background fold + CycleSnapshot.** The counterfactual classified it scale-earned (ADR-0003, ~700ms snapshot builds at 500K). Both subsystem audits grepped consumers: the only production reader was removed at M44.4 Drop A; today the fold goroutine rebuilds an O(N) snapshot up to 4×/sec for a cache only a bench test reads. **Resolution: dead — moved to unforced (~250–300 LOC including the live triple indexes, which also have zero production readers). The counterfactual's classification was stale.**

**(d) Validation-machinery ROI vs tests-as-complexity.** No real conflict — the cross-check passes. The ROI audit found nearly every ladder rung receipted (closed-loop sim: 0.4s repro of bugs that cost 90-minute cloud runs; probes: found the ADR-0040 defect; bench-hot: the #52 ParseQuantity class) and the test audit independently confirms ~51% of test LOC pins paper or incident behaviour. Both converge on the same trims: ~1.2–1.5K of mechanism-level pins (OCC broker mode-matrix ~400, joint-fold agreement tests ~300, five duplicated bench fixtures, M5/M6 milestone-frozen scale files at 432). Both flag bench-hot as a gate with no teeth (no benchstat baseline). Two genuinely split, low-stakes calls, flagged honestly: `cycle_phasedump_test.go` ("justified-defensive, keep" vs "under-justified, delete") — compromise is demoting it to a cmd tool; and fauxctl, where the wide audit disagrees with *itself* ("over-built, replace with a go-test -update flag" vs "proportionate, plan-anchored") — plan-anchored but replaceable for ~200 LOC, low priority either way.

### The under-built side (for honesty's sake)

The wide audit found complexity *missing* where the papers require it, which matters for the verdict's framing — the system's problem is misallocation, not just excess:

- **A22 half-implemented:** the shard→provider fencing edge does not exist on the wire (`provider.proto` carries no `(shard_id, epoch, seq)`; `fencing.Sequence` has zero callers). The package looks complete; only the coordinator→shard half is real.
- **A47/hard-rule inversion:** no provider dial-out client exists; `fake.New` is hardcoded behind a stale "M5 swaps this" comment, so every `helm install bigfleet` deploys the test fixture CLAUDE.md says is never deployed.
- **A16's second half unimplemented:** no Idle→Speculative release path, no Delete action kind, no `provider.Delete` caller — reclaimed surplus ratchets into Idle forever and re-enters the Same acquirable pool, feeding exactly the domain-flip churn ADR-0041/0042 then had to damp.
- **A18 decorative:** "Age > 5 cycles → escalate" gates nothing; every shortfall reports every interval from age 1.
- **A13 silently diverged:** the Idle tiebreak is price-asc, not reclamation_penalty (which is zeroed on drain) — no ADR records the substitution.
- **Headline SLO vacuous:** `pod_bind_latency_steady_seconds` is emitted only by pod-shim; every active profile runs kube-scheduler mode, so the gate silently reads -1 and passes across the entire uber ladder.

## 3. The three largest unforced pools

### Pool 1 — Harness superseded halves: ~1.55–1.65K Go LOC + ~1.55K YAML (+917 MB artifacts)

Three migrations each shipped the new half without deleting the old: profiles (ADR-0034 Stage 7 / M50.7 never landed — 21 profile files for ~6 distinct tests, `uber-5k.yaml` and `5k.yaml` describing the same test in two formats), scheduler modes (ADR-0023 promised pod-shim retirement "once validated"; it is validated for the whole active ladder, yet pod-shim's 773 lines remain the chart default), and demand models (the legacy single-shape mode survives only for dev-50/dev-500; M59 is the fix). Evidence chain that this pool is actively harmful, not just dead weight: the 2026-06-11 dev-50 stall (4,800 slots vs a 4,950 gate) was caused by the legacy-mode seam itself, and was answered with ~400 new lines of preflight/plateau machinery defending a mode already scheduled for deletion; the validation ladder's lower rung structurally cannot catch the catalog/gang/fold failure class where every recent pathology lived; and the headline bind-latency SLO is unmeasured because the deprecated mode owns the canonical metric. The harness is not over-designed — it is over-retained relative to its own ADRs.

### Pool 2 — The ADR-0042 layer and its validation plan: ~500 engine LOC + a share of ~1.6K test LOC

Chooser rule 5, the aged-parking ledger (8/32 tunables, re-probe, three honor-sites), the SameSatisfiable plumbing through `NeedResult`/`Phase1Result`, the `CapacityNeed.group` wire field, and the cascade-mini plan (M65.1). Evidence chain: `realistic.yaml:237-248` ships physically impossible demand (64–256 rack-coherent whole-GPU nodes vs ~52-machine racks) → `seedZoneRack` fabricates 256-machine rack blocks to host it → ~190 permanently-unsatisfiable gangs flip-flop between identical-total buckets → #55/#56 churn at ~27/sec → rule 5 → residual ~9/sec → parking, the escalation path ADR-0042 had explicitly declined on YAGNI grounds. The density contradiction compounds it: `scaleResourceMap` multiplies `nvidia.com/gpu` by 100, so seeded inventory and the harness's fake Nodes disagree about machine size, and ADR-0041's snapshot-dependent foldability can silently flip multi-machine gangs to "foldable" against phantom 800-GPU machines — corrupting the evidence base the Same ADRs were validated against. The engine-core audit adds the structural diagnosis: parking is the third damping layer on one self-inflicted oscillator (dual supply-attribution, patched by mirroring rules four times since ADR-0027 §5.1 instead of unified). And it sits in tension with the paper: bigfleet.md §16 says priority is the sole throttling mechanism, and §9's designed answer to unsatisfiable demand — shortfall buffer plus coordinator escalation — already exists.

### Pool 3 — Dead scaffolding and speculative surface: ~1.0K LOC across engine/protos/metrics/helm

Zero-caller, zero-consumer, or both — verified by grep in both audits: the orphaned OCC `WorkerPool` (78 + 132 test LOC, superseded at M46.3, never swept); the per-cycle `needIndex` map built then discarded ("reserved for future" — an O(Needs) hot-path allocation at up to 776K entries); the `EffectiveCost` wrapper with no call sites; the inventory fold goroutine + live triple indexes (see §2c); the coordinator Rebalancer emitting `TransferOwnership` instructions that both shard-side handlers no-op (~200 LOC simulating function since M6.4); `MaxActionsPerCycle` (subsumed by ADR-0021 backpressure); ~8 dead wire fields from the pre-discipline M1 proto commit (`Hello.capabilities` — verbatim the future-proofing the working brief bans — `CreateRequest.labels`, `ListFilter.zone/instance_type`, `transition_started_unix_nanos`, the AvailableCapacity atomicity/template fields, utilisation placeholders zero since "M11"); 9 of 40 metric families with zero consumers; dead helm values promising RBAC/PodMonitor/CRD-install behaviour no template implements; the `|| true` that has made `--rebalance-interval=0` a lie since M6.5. None of this is load-bearing; all of it misleads the next reader.

## 4. Simplification sequence

Deletions first, then re-sequencings. Each action: what it removes → what gates it → what breaks if done wrong → which in-flight plans change.

**Action 1 — Sweep the dead scaffolding (now; no gate beyond `make lint && make test`).**
Removes pool 3 (~1.0K LOC + ~210 test LOC), plus: `scaleway-*.yaml` (5 files, 599 lines — doubly dead under the rescinded-Scaleway memory), the M54.2 dead knobs (`loadProfile.mode` 'cr' surface, `ReconcilePerTickCap`), and moves `test/scaletest/results/` (917 MB) out of the repo. Reserve proto field numbers rather than reuse; mark ADR-0003 superseded; switch sim's one `CountByState` call to the snapshot. Breaks if done wrong: reusing a reserved field number; deleting the ADR-0021 metric trio while the persistent pool is still under observation (keep those, delete the two with no recorded justification). Changes no in-flight plan.

**Action 2 — Fix the demand-realism artifacts (now; this *is* M61.3, promoted to the front of the queue).**
Two changes: (1) cap `gpu-training-large`'s `sameRack` groupSizeRange at realistic rack occupancy (≤~16 for this node class) and express 64–256 gangs as Same(zone) — which also exercises a currently-untested realistic path; (2) exclude extended resources (`nvidia.com/gpu`) from density multiplication in `scaleResourceMap`, or pin GPU archetypes to density 1, resolving the density/GPU seed contradiction. Gated by: nothing — a YAML range and one function. Breaks if done wrong: this changes the demand shape under the in-flight uber ladder, so re-baseline uber-5k (mechanism run) before trusting prior numbers; ADR-0041 foldability classifications will shift — that is the point, the current ones are partly against phantom capacity. Changes: **M61.3** becomes this action; **the gpu-training-large archetype** is fixed, not deleted; **the density/GPU seed contradiction** is resolved here; `seedZoneRack`'s block logic needs no code change — realistic inputs yield realistic blocks.

**Action 3 — Surface the vacuous SLO; then build its real source (stopgap now; source before any pod-shim deletion).**

## 5. What to keep and why

The core is the paper's machine and should not be touched on complexity grounds: the cost/victim/drain-grace formulas are byte-for-byte §8, the state machine is §5 transcribed, fencing (the half that exists) is §11, the shortfall buffer implements §9 including the 100-entry bound, and the coordinator is lean. The expensive-looking things above that baseline each carry receipts. The OCC broker reversed a measured 5–15-minute Phase 1 at uber-50k after two constant-factor rescue attempts were tried and reverted (ADR-0029). The ADR-0040/0041 Same layers are correctness, not tuning: strict one-domain acquisition with cross-domain crediting produced a self-sustaining 8–21/sec Bootstrap≈Reclaim equilibrium with Configured inflated +77%, and the exclusive machine ledger was simply false for sub-machine gangs (2,400 demanded where 540 existed; 78% phantom-unsatisfiable at 97.5% bind) — the fold resolves it with the paper's own min_unit primitive. ADR-0039's CR-per-Pod reversed an 84%-of-bound-pods-invisible, 32-machines/sec reclaim cascade; ADR-0036's first-rollup gate stops a shard restart from draining its fleet; the operator's write-avoidance stack reverses Drops B/S/AA one measured regression each; grpcutil's ceiling ended the #3/#4 frozen-NeedsTable reconnect loop. And the validation machinery is the best-receipted complexity in the repo — the probes found the ADR-0040 defect and graded every ADR-0042 verdict, and the closed-loop sim reproduces in 0.4 seconds bug classes that previously cost 90-minute cloud runs. That last one is also what makes §4 safe to execute: the regression net is the asset that lets you delete with confidence.

## 6. Process note

Two patterns let the unforced ~15% accumulate. First, **mechanism-before-realism ordering**: ADR-0042 shipped shard-local suppression state — the escalation path it had itself deferred on YAGNI grounds — while its own consequences section deferred the question of whether the triggering demand could physically exist. Five corrective ADRs in four weeks patched the same oscillator without anyone asking whether the oscillation's fuel (64–256-node rack gangs) was real. Had M61.3 landed first, rule 5, parking, and cascade-mini would likely never have been observed into existence. Second, **migrations and milestones don't sweep**: ADR-0034 Stage 7, ADR-0023's retirement promise, M54.2's knobs, and M46.3's scaffolding all left their superseded halves alive, and each survivor then attracted new defensive machinery of its own (the preflight package is 400 lines guarding a mode whose deletion was already scheduled; the dev-50 stall *was* the seam).

Two cheap guardrails: (1) **a demand-realism check on any ADR whose trigger is harness-observed** — one mandatory paragraph: "does this demand shape exist in production, and at what scope?" — answered before any suppression/escalation/stickiness mechanism ships; if the answer is "the catalog says so", the catalog entry gets audited first. (2) **A deletion clause in the ADR template**: any ADR that supersedes a mechanism, mode, or format names the deletion milestone for the old half in the same document, and that milestone blocks the *next* migration touching the same surface — plus a one-line complexity budget ("this adds N mechanisms / M tunables; what it retires: …"), so "adds state, retires nothing" is at least written down where the author reviews it.
